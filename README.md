# Recorded-salutation prediction from wearable epoch data

Experiment overview, reviewed 22 September 2026. This summary covers the reports for all 15 experiment stages, including local output folders and the newest Garmin studies. Selected logs and scripts were inspected where interpretation needed clarification. No experiments were rerun for this review.

The prediction target is recorded salutation, codes 10 versus 20. Results do not establish biological sex, gender identity, or causal physiological effects. Cohorts and evaluation designs differ across experiments, so their absolute AUROCs are not directly interchangeable. A plain-language synthesis of the full campaign, with the measurement equations, is available as the [22 September meta-report](reports/recorded_salutation_meta_report_2026-09-22.pdf).

## What the results support

The strongest new findings are that **recording patterns and HR values contribute complementary predictive information**, and that **hourly/circadian representations add information beyond the existing Garmin summaries**. The older claim that feature-side improvements were exhausted is too strong. R1b extends this to the day level: per-day circadian shape adds **+0.019** AUROC over daily summaries on individual days, largely independent of within-day coverage, and participant-level aggregation preserves a **+0.010** increment on top of the existing baseline.

The temporal-representation ladder extends this to within-day structure: convolutional kernels over 5-minute bins of each participant's first 40 adequate days add **+0.153** AUROC beyond daily summaries on the same folds, and the within-day value-shuffle control attributes the increment to **where values sit in the day** rather than to value distributions or wear patterns. Per-bin daily profiles at the same 5-minute resolution recover little of it, so bin resolution is not the bottleneck. The predeclared selective-classification analysis converts the gain into the applied currency: at 95% target precision the combined model labels **25.2%** of participants on untouched data (per-class precision 0.956 / 0.937), roughly **×2.0** the R1b baseline's 12.8% — but distribution-free certification at the available calibration size (n_val=579) covers only **8.1%**, and 98%-precision certification is unattainable in all ten allocations. Abstention is not wear-driven: labeled and abstained participants differ in mean heart rate, not observation coverage, so minimum-wear gating is not the binding lever. Whether the signal is physiological (circadian phase/shape) or device-behavioral (wear-time routines) is open; the predeclared probes are night-only arms, activity-window exclusion and importance-by-dilation.

The recording-structure pilot asks the converse of the temporal experiment on the same cohort, folds and window: how much salutation information survives deleting HR values while keeping only the observation mask and per-bin event counts. Recording-only MultiRocket reaches **0.63** test AUROC (**0.55–0.57** from static average profiles alone), and roughly **+0.03** of the temporal increment is attributable to the arrangement of observed/missing stretches beyond each day's preserved count distribution and the average clock profile. Stacking these blocks on the temporal HR model does not improve it (**−0.017** val, negative in 3/3): at this representation the HR model already encodes the recording texture. This qualifies, without contradicting, the pooled-cohort informative-recording finding (**+0.0167**): explicit recording features pay off when the value model cannot already see the wear pattern, and are redundant with a representation that can.

Feature hygiene remains useful for reducing matrix width. The tested feature-selection and feature-engineering methods do not support a general performance improvement. Continuous age is a compact alternative to age groups, but its observed gain does not establish superiority.

The informative-recording experiment supports using observation patterns as predictors. It does not identify an MNAR mechanism or establish performance among participants whose salutation is missing. Its executed protocol is documented in [the informative-recording plan](CODEX_INFORMATIVE_RECORDING_FEATURE_PLAN_2026-09-20.md); [MNAR_PLAN.md](MNAR_PLAN.md) is the earlier proposal and should not be treated as the exact executed design.

## Interpretation qualifications

- **Day-scale results are retrospective sampling results.** The script samples k days **with replacement** across the available record. Arm B additionally uses up to **60 separate baseline days**, also sampled from that participant's record. Its “one-day” performance therefore is not performance with only one day of history, and the scaling curve does not establish performance during the first month. R1b's chronological first-k curve addresses the prospective-window question on the R1b model (first day **0.669**, near-plateau by day 32). See the [day-scale implementation](experiments/dayscale_garmin_2026-09-21/run_dayscale.py).
- **Coverage residualization is a predictive adjustment, not proof of deconfounding.** The R1a-AF claim that approximately 37% of the increment is mediated by composition/activity is not identified by these AUROC differences. Likewise, the larger gain under balanced folds does not establish why it increased. The [R1a implementation](experiments/r1a_circadian_garmin_2026-09-21/run_r1a.py) fits a linear adjustment for hourly coverage shares and total recording volume on training participants.
- **Coverage-at-precision is an operating estimate, not a guarantee.** Validation-selected thresholds sit at the precision boundary, so held-out precision shrinks with the size of the selected set (material on the minority class, whose selected sets are small). The Clopper–Pearson variant is the finite-sample certified counterpart and is deliberately conservative; at the available calibration size (n_val=579) it certifies 8.1% at 95% precision and nothing at 98%.
- **Alloc-0-frozen hyperparameters carry selection optimism.** The temporal ridge α was chosen on alloc-0 validation in each run; the extended-grid addendum attributes real headroom to the convolutional arms (Combined +0.0152 val / +0.0159 test at α=10⁴) but shows the Profile288 val gain is selection noise. A v3 rerun with the extended grid is pending decision.
- **The recording-structure pilot is a 3-allocation pilot on reused folds, by design.** Its deltas carry no interval estimates; the arrangement increment (+0.03) rests on MultiRocket applied to binary/count sequences (degenerate bias quantiles), and the position shuffle breaks local order and clock alignment jointly, so their contributions are not separated. The declined HYDRA confirmation was the predeclared robustness check for the transform-degeneracy part of this caveat.
- **The comparison designs differ.** V1/v2 and the original sidequest used optimized allocations; feature selection, engineering, age and original R1a used repeated label-stratified random splits. R1a-AF uses demographic balancing. These remain evaluations on reused cohorts, not independent validation. Repeated-split intervals describe split/model randomization conditional on the observed cohort.
- **The September 20 meta-report is superseded.** The [September 22 meta-report](reports/recorded_salutation_meta_report_2026-09-22.pdf) is the current synthesis (14 stages, through the temporal-representation experiment, selective classification and the regularization addendum, with the measurement and certification equations). The [September 20 report](reports/recorded_salutation_meta_report_2026-09-20.pdf) is retained for the corrected simultaneous intervals for feature selection/engineering, which supersede the older reports' mislabeled intervals and are not reproduced in the new synthesis. R1a and day-scale reports also contain explicit errata.

## Research direction

The work so far emphasizes controlled ablations, paired comparisons and reproducible evaluation. Several summary-feature extensions produced small gains; recording patterns and circadian representations provided more useful increments. The next phase will test assumptions about representation and generalization.

- **Ask a discriminating question.** Each experiment should state its hypothesis, competing explanations, and which outcomes would change the next research decision. The immediate question is whether within-day structure adds information beyond HR distributions and recording patterns.

- **Compare representations under matched conditions.** Hold participants, observation windows and evaluation splits fixed. Compare hourly profiles, five-minute profiles and temporal transforms, including comparisons with the same learner to isolate representation gains.

- **Separate exploration from confirmation.** Reused cohorts and allocations support development and conditional stability checks. After selecting a candidate, freeze the pipeline and evaluate on untouched participants. Additional splits of the existing cohort do not provide independent confirmation.

- **Keep interpretations proportional to evidence.** Distinguish observed predictive gains from proposed mechanisms. Residualization does not establish causal independence or mediation. A negative result applies to the tested configuration; it does not establish a performance ceiling.

- **Define advancement decisions before running.** Specify the gain, stability and computational cost that would justify further investment. Prioritize participant-level AUROC during representation development, then evaluate calibration and precision–coverage trade-offs for high-confidence labeling with abstention. Performance among participants with observed salutation does not establish performance where salutation is missing.

## Cohort allocation package

[`cohort_allocator`](cohort_allocator/README.md) is the separate reusable OR-Tools package. It accepts a participant-level demographic summary, variables to balance, optional independent partitions, and relative fold sizes, then exports assignments, prepared cohorts and balance diagnostics. R1a-AF is the completed experiment demonstrating its use for the later feature comparisons.
